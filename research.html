Microbial communities
genes to ecosystems.

We study which microorganisms are present in a community, how their populations change and how community structure relates to host or environmental conditions. The work combines metagenomics, comparative genomics, numerical ecology and method development.

Microbial diversityMicrobial evolutionMicrobial communities

From a mixed sample to ecological insight.

Microbiomes contain a vast number of organisms, genes and interactions. Rather than treating that complexity as noise, we build ways to resolve it: connecting reads to genomes, genomes to strains, and strains to the ecological processes that shape them.

We work across human, soil and environmental microbiomes, with a particular interest in how microbial variation can inform health, disease and a more precise understanding of ecosystem function.

01

Microbial diversity

Finding the organisms and genetic potential that remain uncatalogued.

Much of microbial diversity remains invisible to standard reference collections. We use metagenome-assembled genomes, marker-gene references and high-resolution strain analysis to identify organisms in human and environmental samples, then place their genes and functions in biological context.

This work moves microbiome research beyond broad taxonomic labels towards the organisms and variants most relevant to a person, population or ecosystem, while recognising how sampling, extraction and reference choice shape what can be detected.

Methods in focus

  • Metagenome-assembled genomes
  • Strain delineation
  • Taxonomic annotation
  • Functional profiling
02

Microbial evolution

Watching genomes change on ecologically meaningful timescales.

Microbial populations are not fixed entities. They gain and lose genes, diversify within hosts and disperse among people, places and habitats. We investigate how these processes affect persistence, transmission and adaptation.

By combining strain-resolved metagenomics, comparative genomics, population genetics and phylogenetic analysis, we ask what a microbial species means in practice, and when variation within a species changes the conclusions we draw about health or ecology.

Questions we ask

  • Which strains persist within hosts?
  • How do bacteria disperse?
  • When do genomes acquire new functions?
  • How does variation relate to health?
03

Microbial communities

Making patterns in complex communities easier to see and test.

Communities are more than lists of species. Their structure reflects shared niches, environmental pressures, host biology and interactions between organisms. We use numerical ecology, matrix factorisation and metabolic models to identify recurring patterns in the gut, soil and water.

These patterns make large datasets more interpretable without forcing every sample into a discrete type. They help us investigate community-level variation associated with diet, inflammation, ageing and broader environmental change.

Systems of interest

  • Human gut microbiomes
  • Diet and complex disease
  • Soil and water communities
  • Community-scale metabolic models
